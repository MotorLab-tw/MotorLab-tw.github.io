0. Read First — Safety

Read this chapter before first use. The user bears sole responsibility for any injury, property loss or machine damage caused by use that ignores these safety notes, which is also not covered by warranty.

🔴 Power safety

  • Use a DC 5V / 6A or higher, safety-certified power adapter.
  • Using an out-of-spec adapter (over-voltage, poor quality, uncertified, unknown origin or modified) will damage the machine and risks fire; resulting faults or damage are not covered by warranty.
  • Stop using if the power cable or connector is damaged, hot or smells odd.

🔴 Operation safety (high-speed rotation)

  • While the motor is running, never touch the shaft, gears or reflector with hands, tools or any object — high-speed rotation can cut, entangle or fling parts.
  • Confirm the motor is firmly mounted on the jig before running; an unsecured motor may fly off.
  • Keep face, hair, loose clothing and jewellery away from a running motor.
  • The motor and nearby parts get hot after running — do not touch bare-handed right after stopping.
  • If you notice abnormal noise, smell, smoke or sparks, cut the power immediately and inspect.

🔴 Environment safety

  • Not waterproof: keep away from water, damp and rain; do not let liquids (water, oil, drinks) splash onto the body or circuitry.
  • Place on a flat, solid, heat-resistant, non-flammable surface; never run it on carpet, paper or flammable materials.
  • Keep the surroundings ventilated; do not cover the vents; do not run for long in a sealed, hot space.
  • Keep away from fire, heat sources and direct sunlight.

🔴 Handling safety

  • Children must be supervised by an adult; parts and motors contain small items and sharp edges.
  • Do not disassemble or modify the machine or circuit; do not drive non-specified motors or loads.
  • The terminals are only for connecting a motor — do not connect batteries, power or other loads, to avoid short-circuit damage.
  • Unplug the power when not in use for a long time.

⚠ Measurement data note

  • Data is only for before/after and relative comparison on the same machine; readings differ systematically across devices/instruments — do not compare directly with others or market figures.
  • Do not use conductive break-in oil during measurement (it distorts readings; see "5. Break-in").

The machine has multiple automatic safeguards — overheat protection, over-current / short-circuit / stall / off-jig detection, soft start/stop (see "16. Safety & Protection") — but these are aids and cannot replace safe operation and supervision.

4. Motor Characterization

Automatically measures a motor's characteristic parameters and losses, giving reference data — no scoring, no ranking. Compare same-model motors and judge for yourself. The system runs 3 consecutive passes and averages them, about 5 minutes, gentle enough for brand-new un-run motors.

How to run: Home → "Motor Characterization" → pick direction (default forward) + pick motor model (16-model dropdown) + add a note (optional, up to 40 chars) → "Start" → fully automatic (shows pass number and progress); do not touch the motor → on completion it shows the data table and auto-saves to the measurement log.

Reading the data (each row is labeled with its direction):

  • ★ I0 (no-load current): at the same speed, lower is better — the most repeatable metric and the primary one for grading motors.
  • ★ Km (quality factor) / ★ T_loss (loss torque): supporting metrics (higher Km is better, lower T_loss is better).
  • Ke (magnetic strength): no absolute good/bad — only meaningful when comparing motors with similar values (higher = torque-leaning, lower = speed-leaning).
  • KV (speed constant, rpm/V): converted from Ke — KV × voltage ≈ no-load speed (e.g. KV 14000 × 3V ≈ 42000 RPM), handy for comparing with published ratings.
  • R (internal resistance): lower is better.
  • Startup voltage: reference only — highly random in a single run; best used to track one motor over time (a clear rise later = bearing / brush wear warning).
  • Loss-fit R² / Ke drift: data-quality indicators (closer to 1 and lower drift = more trustworthy).
  • 3-pass consistency: the max spread of I0 across passes; a warning flag means the motor is unstable or the mounting is loose, so treat that reading as reference only — check and re-measure.

Measurement log: Measurement page → "Log." Every successful run is saved automatically (all metrics and per-speed-point data), up to 50 entries; the oldest is deleted when full. You can view details or delete.

Suggested use:

  • Measure a batch of new motors one by one → pick the good ones by ★I0 before investing break-in time.
  • After break-in, re-measure the same motor: loss metrics should drop (break-in worked); Ke / R should barely change.
  • Compare motors at similar temperatures; the first reading after mounting or a cold restart may run high — rely on the second onward.
Safety protection (fully automatic): during any motor-driving program (break-in / test / characterization / bearing / brush / health check) the machine monitors continuously and stops immediately with an explanation on any anomaly — no motor detected (< 3 s), motor stalled (< 5 s), no speed signal (< 4 s), output short (emergency stop < 0.15 s), overheat (immediate stop + lock). On trigger the buzzer sounds 4 long beeps. Power spec DC 5V / 6A, do not go below; when supply is insufficient an amber warning appears at the top.
Note: a failed run (motor didn't start / lock timeout / overcurrent) shows the reason — just retry. If it keeps failing, or speed reads non-zero while idle, check the alignment of the speed sensor and reflector.

5. Break-in (Two Modes)

Long, low-speed running that beds the brushes to the commutator. The top of the break-in page has two mode buttons (cannot switch while running).

ModeBest forIn one line
Voltage modePlayers with their own recipeFixed voltage per stage (the classic way)
Speed mode (Beta)Players who prefer an RPM targetAuto speed-control to your target; advances when voltage stabilizes
Want "one-tap, fully automatic, stops itself when done"? Use the separate home feature AI Smart Break-in (Pro; see "6. AI Smart Break-in").

Voltage mode

Home → "Break-in Program" → pick motor model (16) + note (up to 40 chars) → pick stop criterion (Time-only = each stage runs its full time; Smart stable = advance early once current settles, time is the cap) → pick passes (1–6, default 1) → confirm the 10-stage parameters for each pass (use the tabs to switch passes; click a number cell to edit) → "Start."

  • Each stage: voltage (0.6–4.0V, 0 = skip) / direction / run seconds (10–600, default 60) / cooling seconds (10–600, default 60) / stable-current tolerance (2–50mA).
  • With multiple passes, "copy this pass to all following" fills the table fast; the estimated total time shows next to the pass count.
  • Out-of-range input is auto-clamped to the limit with a single flash.
Tip: for a brand-new motor the brushes and commutator are not yet shaped — you can tighten the tolerance gradually from loose to tight across passes.

Speed mode (Beta)

Same 10-stage table as voltage mode, but the first column becomes target RPM (6000–30000, steps of 1000, 0 = skip) — the machine adjusts voltage to lock the motor at that speed.

  • Stop criterion (Strict / Standard / Loose): once the drive voltage at that speed is stable, the stage is considered broken-in and advances early; the run seconds act as an upper bound. Strict = needs to be steadier (deeper, longer); Loose = advances faster (switch to it if a brand-new motor never advances).
  • The stage result column shows the stable voltage; if the motor can't reach the target RPM it runs the full time at the safe voltage cap and is flagged "not reached" (usually the target is too high, or the motor / jig is faulty).
  • Direction, time, passes and copy all work the same as voltage mode.

Common behavior

Each stage: soft-start → running → soft-stop → cooling → next stage; after a pass it auto-continues to the next (shows "pass x/y · stage c"). All done → 3 beeps → auto-saved to History (voltage / speed mode, with every pass and stage result).

While running you can press:

ButtonAction
PauseSoft-stops and pauses; the button becomes "Resume"; press again to continue from the breakpoint — seconds already run in the stage are kept (not recounted) and cooling resumes. No time limit on pause; while paused all other buttons are locked, only "Resume" works
StopSoft-stops and ends; record marked "user aborted"
HomeReturns home; break-in keeps running in the background
About pause: while paused the motor stops and cools; resuming is like inserting one extra cooling into that stage — harmless to break-in; the stage's "stability judgment" is re-collected after resuming (to avoid mixing hot/cold states). A power cut does not keep pause progress — cutting power mid-way counts as aborting. ("Reset" is only pressable at idle, clearing the last run's displayed values.)
Note: all settings lock while running (and while paused); don't cut power mid-run (data loss); overheating auto-stops and saves; to bench-check quickly, shorten each stage's time. One extra live field, KV (rpm/V) = current speed ÷ motor voltage, for a quick read on motor state; another, "this stage run x / y s," shows the stage's break-in progress — counting only full-speed seconds (soft-start / soft-stop / pause don't count), advancing at y seconds.
Important: do not use "conductive break-in oil" when measuring / breaking in. Some break-in oils marketed as "conductive" (they contain conductive carbon powder) make RPM jump and voltage drop the instant you add them — that's not real break-in, just an illusion from the carbon temporarily conducting. Every judgment here (stable current / RPM / internal resistance) measures the true brush-commutator contact; conductive oil skews readings optimistically (these oils' own instructions admit "it stabilizes and runs better only after washing") and reverts once washed off. Correct approach: don't use such oils at all; if you already did, wash it off thoroughly before measuring. Normal light bearing lubrication is unaffected — this warning is only about "conductive break-in oil."

6. AI Smart Break-in (Pro)

One-tap fully-automatic break-in: the machine runs, measures and stops itself when done; a built-in Break-in Guide reads across sessions to answer break-in's hardest question — "how many sessions does this motor actually need?"

How to run: Home → "AI Smart Break-in" → pick motor model (all 15 Tamiya) + note (optional, up to 40 chars) + time cap (until done / 2h / 3h / 4h) → "Start," the rest is automatic — the system runs at progressive speeds while measuring, and stops automatically once it detects steady state (no more improvement this session) with a beep.

  • The live data and charts at the top are identical to the break-in page; below it shows: current round / current action / elapsed time / convergence progress, plus a break-in metric curve (per-round points + moving average; a flattening curve = this session near done).
  • The motor alternates forward/reverse (bedding both brush faces symmetrically) and measures periodically; rhythmic changes in sound and speed are normal.
  • Pressing "Stop" stops immediately; data from completed rounds is kept as usual; a manually-stopped session is logged in the guide history but does not count toward the verdict (marked ✗).

Break-in Guide — answering "how many sessions"

One session done = the motor is broken in? Not necessarily. Each auto session stops at its own steady state, but a new motor usually needs several consecutive sessions to truly arrive; after that, more grinding brings no gain and starts consuming brush and magnet life (see "20. Physics": "demagnetization" and "break-in sweet spot"). The Guide's job is to catch the session to stop at.

How it works: after each session the system records its convergence result; the next session is auto-compared with all prior ones — still clearly dropping = still improving; flat for several in a row = improvement exhausted = break-in sweet spot. The verdict shows directly on the panel banner:

BannerMeaningWhat to do
Guide observing — one more session to start judgingNot enough sessions yet to readKeep breaking in normally
Still improving, worth continuing (already saved x mA vs start)This one still has headroomOne more session is worth it
Sweet spot reached, stop recommended — little gain from moreImprovement exhausted, this is itStop; this one is done
Overshot — you're now grinding away life, stop immediatelyThe sweet spot passed a few sessions agoStop now; don't grind this one again

When to look / press:

  1. First session of a new motor: you'll only see "observing" — the first session builds this motor's baseline, which is normal.
  2. Glance at the banner after each session: "still improving" → start another; "sweet spot reached" → stop — this motor's break-in is done, put it in the car.
  3. The "guide record" prompt at start (only when records exist): continuing the same motor → "continue same motor" (default); a different motor is now fitted → "new motor (restart)," which resets the guide record — not resetting means comparing against another motor's record and misjudging.
  4. Forgot to pick new motor? The system compares values and pops an amber reminder when they clearly mismatch this one's history; you can also reset manually via "clear guide record" below the history table (a confirm box appears; clearing is irreversible).

Reading the guide history: each row = one session (newest on top), showing time, model, rounds and that session's convergence value. Read bottom-up: values dropping all the way = still improving; leveling off = sweet spot — this table is the basis for the banner verdict, so you can verify it with your own eyes. Rows marked ✗ (fault or manual stop) don't count toward the verdict; ignore them.

Note: the guide judges per model and starts fresh on a model change; but different units of the same model must be distinguished by picking "new motor" at start. Every motor's sweet-spot session count differs — read the guide verdict, don't memorize a session count. Verdicts are based on same-condition measurement on this machine; the first session's values may shift after a jig change or big temperature change, so break in one more before reading.
After upgrading to v3.6.0: the AI Smart Break-in engine is upgraded and the break-in profile changed, so guide history is not comparable across versions — on the first session after upgrading, pick "new motor (restart)" to rebuild the baseline.

7. Standard Break-in Flow (Before → Break-in → After)

To know "whether break-in actually worked, and by how much," follow this three-step standard flow.

① Measure before: first run AI Torque Prediction (about 1–2 min) and note this motor's internal loss % / constitution % / torque — the pre-break-in baseline.

② Break in (pick one path):

  • AI Smart Break-in: start it and let it stop itself ("broken in to best state"); then read the Break-in Guide banner to decide on another session ("sweet spot reached" = done).
  • Your own recipe: use "Break-in" (voltage / speed mode) with your usual recipe — before/after measurements still compare, so the data tells you exactly how much your recipe achieved.

③ Measure after: run AI Torque Prediction again and compare with before:

MetricEffective break-in looks likeHow to read
Internal loss %Clear dropThe primary metric — less internal friction/loss = genuinely opened up; dropped to a low and no longer falling = fully broken in
Torque (@2.4V / 3.0V)Flat or slightly upTorque is set mainly by the magnet; break-in won't change it much; a clear drop is abnormal — inspect
Constitution %Usually upA composite score; a before/after gap within ±5 is measurement spread — don't over-read a single number
Pre/post-break-in RPM (break-in page)UpHigher RPM at the same voltage = the most direct sign of improved contact
Brand-new note: for a brand-new un-run motor the internal loss % starting point varies by unit and oil condition — in practice some drop clearly after break-in, while others are already clean and low-resistance and stay flat or even rise slightly (larger brush contact area, fresh carbon); both are normal. So for brand-new motors don't read the break-in effect from internal-loss % alone — mainly watch the break-in metric curve dropping and pre/post RPM rising. The "clear internal-loss drop = bedded in" reading applies mainly to used, dirtied or long-stored motors.

④ (Recommended) Build a health fingerprint: break-in complete = this motor's peak state — now open "AI Motor Health Manager" and build a health fingerprint, so periodic checks later show how far it has drifted from peak (see "12. AI Motor Health Manager").

Timing reminder (important): the motor is hot right after break-in, and hot-state readings run conservative (magnetism dips slightly with temperature; oil and carbon haven't settled), so measuring immediately underestimates the effect. Correct approach: note the on-screen "temperature" at the before measurement; after break-in, wait for the temperature to return to the same as before (within ±0.5°C) before the after measurement — same-temperature comparison makes before/after truly comparable.

8. AI Torque Prediction (Pro)

About 1–2 minutes on the machine; using Tamiya's official specs as the baseline, it estimates this motor's torque and constitution score. It answers "what's this one's score" — compared to the official standard, not to other units.

How to run: Home → "AI Torque Prediction" → pick motor model (all 15 Tamiya; pick correctly, or the baseline is wrong) + note (optional, up to 40 chars) → "Start prediction" → fully automatic continuous measurement (about 1–2 min; do not touch the motor) → shows the result page.

Reading the result page:

ItemMeaningHow to read

Constitution vs Tamiya standardTorque as a percent of the official standard (%)~100% = normal constitution; this is innate, break-in can't change it

Motor internal lossSelf-consumption while spinning; lower is betterSee "20. Physics": "motor internal loss" (three states)
Measurement consistency checkWhether this run's three passes are stableTrust it only when it shows "pass"; "fail" means unstable mounting / sensing — re-measure
Status verdict line (bottom of result page)The machine's plain-language conclusion✅ green = internal loss normal, safe to use; ⚠ amber = loss high (break in / clean first) or abnormally low reading (suspect conductive oil — wash and re-measure). The verdict outranks the score

A few things you must know:

  • These are "reference values," torque ±3%, constitution score ±5% — read which band it's in and same-unit before/after changes, don't fixate on single-point decimals.
  • A slightly lower constitution score after break-in than when brand new = normal (a broken-in brand-new motor has a larger brush contact area and slightly higher idle draw) — not damage; for the break-in effect see the table in "7. Standard Break-in Flow."
  • Based on Tamiya's official standard (not an absolute torque-meter value); the official spec is players' shared coordinate, which makes scores comparable in conversation.
  • Different units of the same model scoring differently = normal: it reflects real unit-to-unit variation, not measurement error.
  • Pick the right model: a wrong model = wrong baseline = meaningless score.
Note: fully automatic, don't touch the motor; on "consistency check failed," check motor mounting and speed-sensor alignment before re-measuring; overheating auto-aborts.

12. AI Motor Health Manager (Pro)

Build a health fingerprint for each motor — a snapshot of its peak state at break-in completion — then re-measure periodically so the machine tells you directly "how well it's holding up, and where it's starting to degrade." Compare against itself, not other units.

Correct order (important): first finish breaking in the motor (ideally with AI Smart Break-in to auto-stop), then build the fingerprint. The fingerprint is this motor's best state, and every later check asks "how far from peak now."

  1. Home → "AI Motor Health Manager" → "+ Add motor."
  2. Fill motor model (dropdown, required — diagnosis needs that model's standard) + note (optional).
  3. Confirm → runs about 4.5 minutes to build the fingerprint (3-pass characterization → brush contact → commutation-ripple record). If it warns "may not be fully broken in": this one's internal loss is still high — run AI Smart Break-in first, then rebuild the fingerprint.

Then each card can run:

  • Full check (about 4.5 min): compares magnetism / internal loss / brush contact against the fingerprint — the most complete.
  • Quick check (about 1.5 min): compares internal loss only, for daily screening.

The result page shows a score (0–100, points deducted only for changes "in the bad direction"), a grade (Optimal / Acceptable / Warning / Critical), a trend chart and plain advice. Temperature differences are auto-compensated — a fingerprint built in winter won't misjudge in summer.

When to rebuild the fingerprint: after washing / deep maintenance, or when a check shows "smoother than the fingerprint" — set the new state as the new baseline. The old fingerprint stays in history as this motor's life record.

Note: up to 50 records per motor, 20 motors per machine; during a check don't cut power / press other buttons; overheating auto-aborts; fingerprints built on an old version need one rebuild to use the new diagnosis; on M1 the button is greyed out (tapping prompts to upgrade).

13. Bearing Resistance Test

Measures bearing smoothness; works on any version. Longer time to full stop → smoother bearing.

Home → "Bearing Resistance Test" → pick test voltage (2.4V or 3V) → "Start" → the machine accelerates to that voltage → holds a steady speed 5 s → cuts power directly (no braking) → times to full stop → shows the full-stop time.

Note: the motor's no-load inertia is small, so this test only shows time, no good/bad rating; compare the same motor over time, or different motors, by their times; needs a working speed sensor; test 2–3 times for repeatability.

14. Brush Contact Stability Test

Measures whether brush contact is even; about 35 s, works on any version. Uneven contact → current jitter → CV rises.

Home → "Brush Contact Stability Test" → "Start" (no need to turn the shaft by hand). The flow is fixed at 1V: voltage ramps 0→1V over 10 s → holds 1V for 5 s → samples current at 1V for 20 s → shows the current coefficient of variation CV%.

Reading CV%: CV is the current jitter at fixed voltage — a relative comparison tool, no absolute good/bad line (models differ innately). Suggested uses:

  • Same motor before vs after break-in: CV drops = brushes bedded, contact improved.
  • Same motor tracked over time: CV rising = brush wear / dirty commutator.
  • Motor A vs B relative comparison.
Note: fixed 1V, fixed flow, for fair cross-run / cross-motor comparison; test 2–3 times for repeatability.

20. Physics

Plain-language explanations of the physics terms you'll meet in measurement and break-in — understand what the numbers mean.

TermPlain explanation
Steady state (convergence)The state where the break-in metric stops improving = broken in. There's no fixed number of passes; what matters is whether the metric converges. AI Smart Break-in keeps sensing and only declares a session done at steady state, stopping automatically. Voltage/speed mode only judge whether a "single stage" is stable; overall progress is yours to read. Tip: on a new motor, tighten the stable tolerance from loose to tight across passes — loose early to advance through the rough phase, tight on the last pass to confirm "really stable."
Break-in sweet spotA motor's best stopping point — "improvement just exhausted, wear not yet begun." Break-in gains aren't infinite: every early session improves noticeably, then after some session the gains hit zero — grinding on only adds brush wear and demagnetization (see "demagnetization"), giving the gained performance back bit by bit. The sweet spot differs per motor and can't be memorized as a fixed count or time — the Break-in Guide (see "6. AI Smart Break-in") catches it via cross-session comparison: flat for several = arrived, and the banner tells you "stop recommended."
Stop criterionThe basis for "what counts as stable, when to move on" per break-in stage (selectable on the break-in page). "Smart stable" judges stability automatically, so you don't estimate time yourself.
Stage advanceMoving from one break-in stage to the next. When the stop criterion is met it advances automatically — how fast it advances is itself a signal of break-in progress.
Stable voltageThe motor's actual voltage at the moment a stage is judged stable. Comparing its change across passes under the same conditions is quantitative evidence of progress.
Stable toleranceThe fluctuation range allowed to judge "stable enough" — smaller tolerance = stricter (see the "steady state" tip for setting it).
Startup voltageThe minimum voltage to start the motor from rest. Lower = smoother inside.
Commutation sparkThe tiny arc when the brush switches current on the commutator. It's simultaneously a source of noise, loss and magnet degradation — the smoother the contact, the smaller the spark.
DemagnetizationThe magnet weakening. Magnetism isn't "used up" (it wouldn't drop sitting for a century) — it's "knocked out": each commutation spark = a tiny reverse-field blow, and over long high-speed running (especially with forward/reverse) billions accumulate, so magnetism weakens irreversibly bit by bit — the RPM then looks faster, but that's the illusion of weaker magnetism, not more strength. Stop at the sweet spot (the AI Smart Break-in guide tells you); every extra minute is life consumed.
False conduction (conductive-oil illusion)Carbon powder temporarily conducting makes numbers look especially nice — an illusion, not real break-in. The machine's judgments are rooted in the motor's true electrical state; conductive oil skews readings optimistically — wash it off before measuring (see the "5. Break-in" warning).
KV valueCurrent RPM ÷ the motor's actual voltage (rpm/V), a quick indicator of motor state — at the same voltage, higher means faster.
Motor internal loss (no-load loss)The power the motor consumes just spinning (brush friction + bearing drag) — think of an engine's idle fuel use: consumed before it even pushes the car. Lower is better: low internal loss = more power actually driving the car. The percent baseline = that model's "standard internal loss" (from official spec) as 100% (e.g. 30% means about a third of the standard baseline; one ruler across models). Read three states, not a single point: (1) still dropping = break-in still improving (new ~30% → broken-in ~26%); (2) dropped and flat = hit this motor's floor = broken in (brushed Dash-series reference: ~30% new, ~25–27% broken in); (3) rose after bottoming = brush / commutation-face aging or over-grinding warning. It's not "lower forever"; the floor is set by the motor's structure. Abnormally low? Suspect measurement or false conduction first (see that term) and re-measure.
Constitution (% of standard)One AI-torque-prediction output: this unit's innate torque as a percent of Tamiya's official standard, ~100% being normal. It reflects the innate winding and magnet condition — break-in can't change constitution (strength is innate; break-in changes how thrifty it is).
Efficiency scoreAn AI-torque-prediction composite: combining "output" and "power draw" into one cross-unit-comparable efficiency figure — higher means the same strength costs less power.
True currentEquivalent current converted from no-load measurement to the standard load condition, for comparison with Tamiya's official current band. Plainly: "how much this one is estimated to draw under official conditions."
Reference RPMThe baseline RPM measured each round of Smart Break-in under fixed identical conditions. With conditions fixed, cross-round change is purely the motor itself changing — that's its purpose.
Natural driftThe same motor reads slightly differently each time — normal. Brush contact and temperature cause small drift; read the trend and multi-run average, don't compare single points.
Systematic difference (measurement baseline)Why your data won't match others'/the internet's: different voltage baselines, supply, jigs and methods all cause an overall offset; before-vs-after on the same machine is the most reliable.
Bearing resistanceMechanical friction between shaft and bearing — one mechanical component of internal loss. Dedicated test: "13. Bearing Resistance Test."
Brush contact stabilityHow stable the brush-commutator contact is — flickery contact makes the numbers jump. Dedicated test: "14. Brush Contact Stability Test."
RippleTiny fast fluctuations on the current — the "fingerprint" of commutation and contact quality; flatter = better-bedded brushes.
